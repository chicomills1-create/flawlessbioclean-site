THE SHORT ANSWER

A hoarding cleanup with Flawless Bio Clean moves at a respectful pace: a private first conversation, a walkthrough of the home together, sorting side by side so nothing important is lost, removal of what is ready to go, then sanitizing and restoring the home to a safe, livable standard. THE DETAILS

The full picture.

Step one: the first call

It starts with a conversation, and the conversation is private. People call us for themselves, for a parent, for a tenant, sometimes for a home they have inherited — and almost everyone who calls says some version of the same thing: I don't know where to start. Starting is our job. On the call we talk through the home, what the priorities are, and what a good outcome looks like to you. There is no judgment in it and no pressure attached. Hoarding cleanups are part of the biohazard work owner Yvette Valenti began her career in, and this team has seen homes at every stage. Nothing about yours will shock us.

Step two: the walkthrough

Before any work is scoped, we walk the home together — the person who lives there, family if they want to be there, and our team. The walkthrough sets the priorities: safe pathways, working kitchens and bathrooms, sleeping spaces, access to doors and utilities. It also surfaces what matters most to the person who lives there — the belongings that are genuinely important — so those are protected from the very beginning. The scope and the order of work come out of this visit, confirmed with you before anything begins.

Step three: sorting together

This is the heart of the work, and it is done together, not to someone. Belongings are sorted side by side — what stays, what goes, what needs a decision later. Important documents, photographs, keepsakes and valuables are set aside carefully as they surface, never swept into a pile. Decisions stay with the client at every step; the team's role is to carry the physical work and keep the process moving at a pace that stays manageable. Some homes take one visit. Some take several, room by room. Either is fine — the pace is part of the plan, not a problem with it.

Steps four to six: removal, sanitizing, restoring

What is agreed to go, goes — removed from the home and disposed of responsibly. Then the cleaning itself: kitchens and bathrooms brought back to a sanitary standard, surfaces cleaned, the accumulated effects of a home that has been hard to maintain addressed room by room. The goal is not a showroom. The goal is a home that is safe, livable and maintainable — clear pathways, working rooms, and a standard the person living there can keep. For many clients, our recurring maid service afterward is what keeps it there, on a schedule that supports the progress instead of letting it slide.

People reach out at different moments. Sometimes it is the person who lives in the home, ready for a change and unsure how to begin. Sometimes it is an adult child who has watched a parent's home fill over the years and wants help that treats their parent with dignity. Sometimes it is a landlord, a property manager, or a family settling an estate. The situation differs; the process does not — private, patient, and built around the person the home belongs to. Whoever makes the call, the first conversation is the same: we listen, we explain the steps, and the decisions stay where they belong.

Will you throw things away without asking?

No. Sorting happens together, and decisions about belongings stay with the client. The team's work is the lifting, carrying and cleaning — not deciding what someone's possessions mean. Items of importance are set aside carefully as they are found.

How long does a hoarding cleanup take?

It depends on the size of the home, how much has accumulated, and the pace the person who lives there is comfortable with. That is exactly what the walkthrough establishes — the scope, the order of work, and a pace that is part of the plan from the start.

Is the process private?

Yes. The work is discreet and non-judgmental, handled by an owner-led, in-house team — never subcontracted crews. What the team sees in a home stays in the home.
